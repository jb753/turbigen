Spanwise flow¶

Spanwise distribution of the flow the blades are designed against.

The mean line holds one value of each flow variable per station, at the root-mean-square radius. A blade needs a flow angle at every span fraction, because its metal angle is a recamber measured off the local flow, so something has to say how the flow varies from hub to casing. That is this stage, read from the spanwise: key of a config.

There are two alternatives.

VortexExpon is a power-law swirl distribution, \(V_\theta \propto r^n\), with the meridional velocity uniform over the span. It is the default, with \(n=-1\), a free vortex.

VelocitySpline holds a spanwise profile of meridional velocity and of angular momentum at every station, the inlet and outlet of each row, as values at that row’s blade sections:

  • The meridional velocity is a shape, rescaled to an area average of one at each station so that the mean line keeps the mass flow,

    \[V_m(s) = V_{m,\mathrm{rms}}\, \frac{f(s)}{\int_0^1 f\,r\,\mathrm{d}s \big/ \int_0^1 r\,\mathrm{d}s} \,.\]

  • The angular momentum is an additive offset \(\Delta\) from the mean line’s own, in units that do not vanish when the mean line has no swirl,

    \[r V_\theta(s) = r_\mathrm{rms} V_{\theta,\mathrm{rms}} + \Delta(s)\, r_\mathrm{rms} V_{m,\mathrm{rms}} \,,\]

    so \(\Delta=0\) everywhere is a free vortex at the mean-line swirl, and an axial exit when that swirl is zero.

The knots are the blade sections. A blade reads the flow at its sections to set their metal angles, so a value at each section is exactly what decides the blade; the spline between them, linear beyond the outermost, serves the spans in between, the initial guess and the fits to a solution.

The two kinds of station mean different things. A row inlet holds the flow arriving there, which is a state the CFD measures; the SpanwiseInflow iterator copies it in. A row outlet holds a setting of the blade: with the trailing-edge recamber held at zero, the outlet \(\Delta\) is the trailing-edge metal angle, and differs from the flow that leaves by the deviation. It is moved by SpanwiseWork on a rotor and SpanwiseSwirl on a stator until the CFD meets their targets.

Span fractions are mapped to radius linearly between the mean-line hub and casing radii of each station.

turbigen.spanwise.spline(knots, values)¶

Return the spanwise curve through knot values at span fractions knots.

A natural cubic inside the knots and straight beyond them; a constant for one knot, a line for two. Linear in the knot values, which is what lets fit_knots() fit a measured profile onto exactly the representation the design evaluates.

turbigen.spanwise.fit_knots(knots, spf_meas, y_meas, weights=None)¶

Return the knot values whose curve best fits a measured profile.

Weighted linear least squares, with the curve through each unit knot vector as a basis function. A profile sampled from a curve on the same knots is recovered exactly.

Parameters:

  • knots (array_like, shape (n_knot,)) – Knot span fractions.

  • spf_meas (array_like, shape (n_meas,)) – Where the profile was measured, and its values there.

  • y_meas (array_like, shape (n_meas,)) – Where the profile was measured, and its values there.

  • weights (array_like, shape (n_meas,), optional) – Relative weight of each measurement, uniform by default.

class turbigen.spanwise.SpanwiseDesign¶

Bases: Node

How the flow varies from hub to casing.

A member gives the meridional velocity and angular momentum at any span fraction of any station; the relative flow angle a blade is designed against follows from those here. Stations are numbered as the mean line’s are, k = 2 i_row at a row inlet and one more at its outlet, and knots is the span fraction of each blade section of that row.

See turbigen.spanwise for the alternatives.

rVt(station, k, knots, spf)¶

A spline design gives a profile at all four stations of a two-row machine, here a fan rotor and outlet guide vane whose blades each have sections at span fractions 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8. Each station takes one value per section of its row, a free vortex at uniform meridional velocity where nothing is written:

spanwise:
  type: velocity_spline
  stations:
    - {}                         # rotor inlet
    - {Vm: [0.95, 1.05, 0.98]}   # rotor outlet
    - {Vm: [0.95, 1.05, 0.98]}   # vane inlet
    - {}                         # vane outlet

Every knot is a leaf of the config, so spanwise.stations[1].rVt[2] can be swept by a batch or set from the command line like any other. Adding a section to a blade adds a knot to both its stations, so their lists must grow with it.

Iterating a spanwise design¶

Three iterators under iterate: move the knots of a velocity_spline design:

type:

Knots it moves

Until

spanwise_inflow

Vm at every station and rVt at every row inlet, of the rows it names or all of them

the profiles match the pitch-averaged CFD

spanwise_work

rVt at one rotor’s outlet

the stagnation enthalpy rise along each stream surface matches a work coefficient distribution psi, one value per section

spanwise_swirl

rVt at one stator’s outlet

the swirl leaving matches a distribution rVt, one value per section

The first copies a measured state in. The other two set the trailing edge, so neither can be used with deviation, which recambers every row’s: the outlet knots carry the mean deviation, and the mean work or swirl is corrected along with its distribution. Only the shape of a psi or rVt target is used — its mass average is removed — since the mean is the mean line’s to set.

iterate:
  correct:
    - type: spanwise_inflow
    - type: spanwise_work
      i_row: 0
      psi: [0.03, 0.0, -0.03]
    - type: spanwise_swirl
      i_row: 1
      rVt: [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]

Each is measured on the cut planes the mean line is mixed out on, a little into the gap beside each blade edge, pitch-averaged by mass and fitted onto the section knots by least squares, over the span between the outermost sections. The endwall bands outside them still count towards the mean: an outlet iterator holds the mean of its knots to the mean measured over the whole span, so the row as a whole meets the mean line. The spanwise_targets post-processor draws each measured profile against the target it is driven to.
